Instagram Image Sizes Every Creator Should Know in 2026

Instagram is not a single image format. Stories, feed posts, carousels, and Reels each need a different size and aspect ratio. This post maps the exact dimensions for 2026, flags the most common resizing mistakes, and shows you how to bulk-export a batch of correctly-sized images.

Why Instagram Makes This Complicated

Instagram is a multi-format platform. A story is a vertical clip that fills a phone screen. A feed post is square or landscape and appears in a grid of thumbnails. A carousel is a stack of images that a user swipes through, and each image is displayed at a different size depending on where it sits in the stack. Reels are vertical videos with a still image that acts as the thumbnail. Each of these four formats has a preferred aspect ratio and a recommended pixel dimension, and the difference is the difference between an image that fills the frame exactly and one that is cropped to a smaller safe zone.

The most important practical consequence is that the "right" image for one format is the wrong image for another. An image that is 1080 × 1080 square is perfect for a feed post and useless for a Story, where the device is in portrait mode. A wide image that is 1920 × 1080 is perfect for a feed post but will be cropped to a narrow banner on a Story. The solution is to produce a set of files for each format, and to name them clearly so you do not upload the wrong size by accident.

The Instagram Reference Table

The table below reflects the dimensions Instagram currently recommends for its main formats in 2026. Aspect ratios are included because they are what matter when a user crops or a platform shrinks the image.

Format Dimensions Aspect ratio Best use
Feed post 1080 × 1080 px 1:1 (square) Photographs, product shots
Feed post 1080 × 1350 px 4:5 (portrait) Portrait photography, portraits
Feed post 1080 × 566 px 1.91:1 (landscape) Landscape photography, landscapes
Story / Reel 1080 × 1920 px 9:16 (vertical) Stories, Reels
Carousel 1080 × 1080 px 1:1 Multi-image posts

The Safe Zone, and Why It Matters

Every platform hides its own UI elements over the edges of your image. On Instagram Stories and Reels, the device's notch and the app's controls overlay the very top and the very bottom of the image. A caption that sits in the bottom third of the image will be partially covered. The safe zone is the central area of the image where the content remains visible regardless of the app chrome. The rule of thumb is to keep your focal point inside the central 80% of the image, and to leave the edges clear enough that the UI elements cannot touch your text or product.

This is different from the dimension requirement. The pixels are what the platform requests; the safe zone is where the content must live within those pixels. A correctly dimensioned image that ignores the safe zone will still look broken to a viewer who is scrolling quickly.

A Practical Workflow for a Creator

The most efficient workflow is to produce a single production set and then export each format. Start with the highest-resolution source image, crop it to 1:1 for the feed post, and export it at 1080 × 1080 pixels. Then create a 9:16 version from the same source, keeping the focal point in the centre, and export at 1080 × 1920 pixels for the Story and Reel. Use the Image Resizer's 4:3 and 16:9 presets for the landscape versions. Export all versions to a clearly named folder, and upload the correct file every time.

Summary

Instagram creators should treat format preparation as a batch operation. Starting from one high-resolution source, export a set of correctly dimensioned files for feed posts, Stories, and Reels, keep the focal content inside the safe zone, and verify the result by previewing at the small sizes where most viewers actually see the content.
