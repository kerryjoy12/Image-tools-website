Best Image Sizes for Social Media in 2026

Instagram, Facebook, X, LinkedIn, and Pinterest all have their own preferred dimensions, and they change as platform interfaces are redesigned. This guide consolidates the official recommendations into a single reference table, shows you how to crop to the right aspect ratio, and explains why a good thumbnail can double your click-through rate.

Why Image Size Matters More Than Ever

Social platforms are a visual medium. Users scroll quickly, decisions happen in the first two seconds, and a stretched, blurry, or incorrectly cropped image tells your audience, without words, that you did not care about the details. A crisp, correctly sized image improves perceived professionalism and directly affects engagement — a well-framed thumbnail can increase click-through rates by a meaningful margin.

At the same time, platforms resize images automatically. A 3000-pixel-wide hero image posted to Facebook will be shrunk to a small rectangle on mobile, and the extra pixels are simply wasted bandwidth and storage. Resizing to the platform's recommended dimensions means your content loads faster for the reader and takes up less space in your hosting account.

The Platform Reference Table

The table below reflects the dimensions that major platforms use as their cropping and upload baselines as of 2026. Always check the platform's help centre before an important campaign, because interface changes occasionally update the guidance.

Instagram Stories 1080 × 1920 px 9:16 (vertical)

Pinterest Pin image 1000 × 1500 px 2:3 (vertical)

The Most Common Cropping Mistakes

The single most common issue is a square image uploaded where a landscape is expected, or a wide image where a portrait is needed. The platform will automatically crop to fit, and if the aspect ratio does not match, the crop line will appear in an unexpected place, cutting off the top of a person, the logo, or the important part of a photograph.

The second most common mistake is oversize. If you upload a 5000-pixel-wide image and the platform only needs 1200, you have paid for the extra data and delivered a slower page. Crop to the exact dimensions the platform requires before you upload.

The third mistake is ignoring the safe zone. Every platform hides UI elements over the edges of your image. Facebook shows your cover photo beneath the search bar and the app chrome; Instagram Stories have a notch overlay in the middle; LinkedIn places its interface over the bottom of a profile banner. Always keep the most important visual content inside the central safe area.

Good social media images are a combination of the right dimension, the right aspect ratio, and content placed safely inside the frame.
