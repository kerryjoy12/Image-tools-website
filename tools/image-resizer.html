Image Resizer

100% Client-Side Resize by pixels or percentage, lock the aspect ratio, and use social media presets. No files leave your device.

A high-resolution photo that looks great on a full-screen laptop can be a miserable file to share by email. PixelTools Image Resizer gives you complete control over the exact dimensions of your output image. Choose to resize by a fixed pixel width and height, or by a percentage of the original. Turn on Lock aspect ratio to stop your photo from turning into a stretched oblong, or pick from one of five presets for Instagram, YouTube thumbnails, Facebook covers, passport photos, and 1080p video frames.

How to Resize an Image

  1. Upload files. Drag & drop or click to browse. You can also paste an image from your clipboard. Multiple files can be resized in a single batch.
  2. Pick a dimension method. Use Pixels to set an exact width and height, or Percentage to scale by 25%, 50%, 150%, and so on.
  3. Lock the aspect ratio. Keep this checked to preserve the original proportions. Uncheck it only if you deliberately want to stretch or squash the image.
  4. Select a preset. Instagram posts (1080×1080), YouTube thumbnails (1280×720), Facebook covers (1920×1080), passport photos (600×600 px with a 2×2 cm reference), or 1080p video (1920×1080).
  5. Review and download. The result preview updates instantly, then you download each resized image individually or all as a ZIP archive.

Understanding the Presets

  • Instagram post — 1080×1080 pixels is the official square post size. It keeps file sizes small and looks sharp on every phone screen.
  • YouTube thumbnail — 1280×720 pixels. This is the maximum recommended resolution for YouTube thumbnails and ensures your text and branding stay crisp.
  • Facebook cover — 1920×1080 pixels. The ideal cover-browser ratio for both desktop and mobile news feeds.
  • Passport photo — 600×600 pixels. Government biometric requirements usually call for a square headshot around 2×2 inches. Use this preset with a 300-DPI mental check for print.
  • 1080p video frame — 1920×1080 pixels. Standard full HD. Undistorted and universally compatible.

Tips for the Best Results

  • Never upscale a small photo wildly. A 640×480 image cannot gain detail by being enlarged to 3000×2000. The result will look soft and pixelated. Use the resizer for downscaling or modest upscaling only.
  • Keep the aspect ratio locked. This protects your composition. Free resizing is fine for creative crops, but it is almost always a mistake when preparing a photo for a strict platform or document.
  • Choose the right output format. The resizer keeps the original format by default. If the resized file still contains a lot of unused metadata, try the JPG to PNG converter or WebP tool afterwards for a smaller final file.
  • Batch workflow. Drag in fifteen course screenshots or product photos and resize them all to the poster size in one click.

Use Cases

Creating thumbnails for a YouTube channel, building a photo gallery for a website, preparing passport and visa documents, resizing screenshots for documentation, and shrinking mobile uploads before sharing them with a client.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does resizing change the quality of my image?

✓

Resizing changes the pixel dimensions, not the colour depth or compression. If you downscale a large image, the file can actually become smaller because fewer pixels are stored. If you upscale, the image gets bigger but does not gain detail — the quality stays the same as the original.

Are the social media presets correct for 2026?

✓

Yes. The Instagram, YouTube, and Facebook presets reflect the dimensions those platforms currently recommend for crisp, full-bleed displays. Platform defaults change occasionally, and the preset button lets you update quickly if they do.

Can I resize by percentage instead of pixels?

✓

Yes. Choose the Percentage tab and enter any value from 1% to 400%. The aspect ratio lock stays active, so your photo keeps its proportions.

What happens if I unlock the aspect ratio?

✓

The image will be stretched to fill the exact width and height you enter. Only use this if you intentionally want distortion — for example, to fit a custom-shaped frame or a design mockup.

Can I resize a large batch of images at once?

✓

Yes. Upload a folder of images and resize them all to the same preset. The tool processes them in the browser and gives you a ZIP download with every resized file.

Remember

  • Keep originals: Always keep a backup of the unresized original before overwriting it.
  • Watch metadata: Resizing does not remove EXIF data. Use the converter tools if privacy requires it.
  • Match the format: Resize first, then convert to WebP for the smallest file size.
