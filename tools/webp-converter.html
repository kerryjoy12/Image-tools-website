WebP Converter

100% Client-Side Convert to and from WebP with a quality control slider. The modern, efficient image format — free, private, no uploads.

WebP is the image format Google developed to replace JPEG, PNG, and GIFs on the modern web. It stores photographs at a fraction of the file size of JPG while preserving the same visual quality, and it supports full transparency like PNG. The browsers that matter today — Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari 14 and later — all render WebP natively. PixelTools WebP Converter lets you produce WebP files from any source and to convert WebP back to a more compatible format, all with a quality control slider so you stay in charge.

How to Convert to or from WebP

  1. Upload a file. Drag & drop a JPG, PNG, or WebP onto the upload zone. Click to browse, or paste an image from your clipboard.
  2. Choose the direction. Convert JPG to WebP to shrink a photograph, or PNG to WebP when your PNG has photographic content that does not need transparency.
  3. Adjust the quality slider. Move the slider between 0 and 100. Lower values produce smaller files at the cost of some detail; higher values are near-lossless. A value of 80–85 is the recommended sweet spot for photography.
  4. Download the result. Your converted file is ready instantly, with no server round trip.

Why WebP Is Better Than JPG for Photos

JPG has been the web standard for decades, but it is remarkably wasteful. It discards information it assumes the human eye will not notice, and it does so in ways that build up noise and banding over time. WebP uses modern prediction and entropy coding to keep far more of that information while still producing a smaller file. At equivalent visual quality, you can expect WebP to be 25–35% smaller than a well-compressed JPG. That matters for page speed, for bandwidth costs, and for the user experience on mobile connections.

Using the Quality Slider

The tool's quality slider behaves the same way for JPG, PNG, and WebP sources. Drag it and watch the preview update. At 90–100 the difference between the original and the converted file is imperceptible. At 70–80 you will notice the file is much smaller and the detail is marginally softer — perfect for images displayed at small sizes on a website, or for background images where file size is the priority.

Use Cases

Optimising photography for a fast-loading website, delivering WebP variants to modern browsers, shrinking a PNG screenshot that photographs had become because of its size, archiving collections of images, and preparing image-heavy assets for a WordPress or e-commerce platform that supports the format.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is WebP supported by all browsers?

✓

WebP is supported by all browsers with substantial market share today: Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari 14+. Safari 13.1 added support, and Safari 14 is the cutoff for the oldest version. For older devices, the JPG or PNG converter is the safe fallback.

Does WebP support transparency?

✓

Yes. WebP supports full alpha transparency, matching PNG. When you convert a PNG that has transparency, the output keeps the alpha channel intact.

Can I convert WebP back to JPG?

✓

Yes. Switch the direction toggle to convert WebP to JPG. This is useful when you need to share images with people on older devices that cannot display WebP.

What quality should I use?

✓

Start at 82. If the image still looks good, you can lower it to 75 to save more space. If you see artefacts, lift it back up. The slider shows the estimated file size live, which makes the choice obvious.

Can I convert a batch of images to WebP?

✓

Yes. Upload a folder of images and each one is converted to WebP. All results download as a ZIP archive.

Before You Convert

  • Check the preview: Always zoom the result to 100% before trusting a quality setting.
  • Keep the original: Store a JPG or PNG backup, because WebP is a newer format with narrower older-browser support.
  • Mixed source: Uploading a PNG that is photographic will shrink better as WebP than as PNG.
