Image Cropper

100% Client-Side Drag-to-crop with fixed ratios, rotation, and flip. A fast, private online image cropper. No files ever leave your device.

Cropping is where the design of an image is decided. A square crop makes a perfect profile picture, a 16:9 crop gives you a clean banner, and a 4:3 crop might be exactly what a portfolio or a school project needs. PixelTools Image Cropper gives you professional crop controls in a browser that runs entirely on your own device.

Drag any corner or edge of the crop box to select the area you want to keep. Toggle the fixed ratio presets to snap the crop to a specific relationship, rotate the image to correct a tilted horizon, and flip it horizontally or vertically when you need a mirror orientation. The result preview and the download button update in real time, and the original file is never modified.

How to Crop an Image

  1. Upload a file. Drag & drop a JPG, PNG, or WebP onto the upload zone, click to browse, or paste from your clipboard.
  2. Draw the crop box. Click and drag anywhere on the image to open a draggable selection rectangle. Fine-tune the edges by dragging the handles.
  3. Choose a ratio. Pick 1:1 for square thumbnails, 4:3 for classic framing, 16:9 for banners, or free mode to draw any shape.
  4. Rotate or flip. Use the controls to tilt the image, flip it horizontally or vertically, or reset to the original orientation.
  5. Download. The cropped result is ready in one click. It is cropped to exactly the dimensions of the crop box, with no extra whitespace.

How to Choose the Right Ratio

  • 1:1 (square) — Instagram posts, profile pictures, app icons. A compact, equally balanced shape that works on every platform.
  • 4:3 — Classic photographs, document headers, email banners. Slightly taller than a square, making it a good compromise between landscape and portraiture.
  • 16:9 — YouTube thumbnails, site banners, slideshows. A modern landscape canvas that fills a wide display without cropping the sides.
  • Free — Handy when you simply want to trim away excess background without imposing a strict shape.

Tips for Better Crops

  • Zoom before you crop. The crop handles become small on low-resolution images. Zoom in on a detail, then crop precisely around the subject.
  • Set the composition before resizing. Crop first, then resize down. If you resize a too-tight crop down to a different size, you will only lose detail.
  • Keep the subject centred in the rule of thirds. Many alignment guides in cropping tools follow this principle. Place your subject near an intersection rather than dead centre for a more dynamic portrait.
  • Watch the rotation guide. When correcting a tilted horizon, use the rotation slider and watch the building edges line up.

Use Cases

Turning a screenshot into a clean square for an avatar, trimming banners and headers for social media, removing unwanted people or objects from the edges of a photo, correcting the orientation of a rotated photo, and creating a landscape image from a portrait frame.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the cropper keep the original dimensions?

✓

The original file is never changed. The tool creates a new image from the cropped region, and the download is a separate file. The original remains on your device untouched.

How do I crop a specific ratio?

✓

Select 1:1 for square, 4:3 for classic, 16:9 for landscape, or Free to draw any shape. The crop box snaps to that ratio as you drag.

Can I rotate or flip an image before cropping?

✓

Yes. Use the rotation slider for fine adjustments or 90-degree steps, and the flip buttons for horizontal and vertical mirroring. These work on the full image before the crop is applied.

Is the cropped image high resolution?

✓

The output keeps the full resolution of the source image, minus the removed border. If the original is 4000×3000, a small 4:3 crop still leaves you with a 4000×3000 file.

Can I crop a batch of images with the same ratio?

✓

Yes. Upload multiple images and choose a ratio; each one is cropped individually and downloaded as a ZIP archive.

Before You Crop

  • Zoom first: View the crop area at 100% so you can place the handles precisely.
  • Check the compass: Use the rotation tool before cropping to avoid re-cropping after a correction.
  • Keep the original: Always keep a backup copy of the un-cropped image in case you need a different framing later.
